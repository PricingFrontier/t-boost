## root_cause
The cap does exactly what the code computes, and the docs promise more than that. `mse_cap = cap * D * sum(w) / sum(h)` bounds only the second-order term `sum(h*delta^2)/sum(w) <= cap*D`: the deviance the banding move would add if the unbanded tables were exactly right. Banding holds this on the cross-fitted combined move. The docs and docstrings call it "the most banding may cost, as a fraction of the training deviance", which is false.

**Method.** In 39 base fits, banding's exact training-deviance change was split into `2*sum(g*delta)/sum(w) + sum(h*delta^2)/sum(w) + remainder`, with g and h the NLL gradient and curvature at the unbanded tables. The fits were: 7 fast datasets x splits 0,1 x n_bags 1 and 8 (graduate=False); fremtpl2 split 0 at 1 and 8 bags and split 1 at 1 bag; fr_instal and pg16_fleet x 2 splits x 1/8 bags (softmax gradient, full quadratic form).

**The task's three hypotheses:**
1. **First-order term: yes, it is the whole excess.** It is positive on the training rows in 35 of the 35 fits that moved anything. The realized rise is 0-12.3x the cap; 0-5.9x where the cap binds alone (one bag). fremtpl2 at one bag: 3.88x (split 0, train +0.388%, test 0.304892, the task's numbers) and 3.50x (split 1). It is positive because the bagged, shrunk, early-stopped tables are not at their per-cell training optimum. Banding smooths further and strips noise the tables absorbed, and the training rows count that as a loss. On test rows the same term ranges from -0.240% to +0.405% and is negative in 17 of 39 fits. It decides whether banding helps (swmc and fremtpl2 at one bag) or hurts (ausbi, fr_instal, pg16_fleet).
2. **Second-order approximation: no.** The higher-order remainder is at most 0.025x the cap.
3. **Merges interacting: no.** The budget is held on the combined cross-fitted move (all tables summed per row). The second-order term stays at most 0.87x the cap on training rows and 0.86x on test rows in every fit.

The training rows also misjudge the move where tables are sparse. The distil pins the training rows: on surrender the training-row move is 0.003-0.07x the budget while the test-row move is 0.47-0.92x. So the training rise is a biased yardstick in both directions.

**Second part, reproduced (cap0_behaviour.jsonl, plus the code):**
- Single-output: `band_deviance_cap=0` meant mse_cap = inf. With 8 bags the noise budget acted alone; with 1 bag banding was skipped, leaving only a report key.
- Multiclass: 0 gave every class a zero budget, so the core's "cannot meet its fidelity budget" skip left every class bank unbanded at 1 and at 8 bags. That is a second silent skip.
- inf and NaN were inconsistent too. On the multiclass path at one bag they reach the core as an infinite budget, which bands without bound.

## recommendation
Adopt the patch. `band_deviance_cap` keeps its mechanism but is now described as what it is: the expected (second-order) cost of the banding move, as a fraction of the training deviance. That bound held in every one of the 39 measured fits, on training and on test rows. It does not bound the realized training rise, which is 0-12.3x the cap because of the first-order term.

The cap must be finite and greater than 0 on every path. Anything else raises ValueError at the start of fit, naming the parameter and the one-bag role, which removes the silent one-bag skip and the multiclass zero-budget skip.

Do not include the first-order term, calibrate to the realized training rise, add OOB pricing, drop the cap or retune it. Calibration is dominated at matched readability by a plain band_tolerance change, is worse on fremtpl2 at 2.5-10x the cells, and binds at every bag count. Dropping the cap leaves one-bag fits unbounded and costs fr_instal about 0.10%. The cap level's test response is flat.

No model bytes change for valid settings.

## risks
- **cap=0 now raises.** Anyone who used it as "no cap" with bags gets a ValueError. haute never passes the parameter (read-only grep). The equivalent setting is any finite cap above the noise budget, e.g. 1.0, but at n_bags=1 that means nearly unbounded banding.
- **The isfinite(dev_mean) guard is gone.** dev_mean is a weighted mean of finite unit deviances over a positive total weight, so it is finite for every input ingest accepts. If a NaN ever reached it, Rust's `f64::min` ignores NaN and a one-bag fit would band without bound. I chose not to add an unreachable defensive raise.
- **The new contract test checks an in-sample property** of the deployed banding. The core holds the budget on the cross-fitted estimate, not in-sample. The margin is 0.19x / 0.56x of the cap on the fixture and at most 0.87x across 39 panel fits: robust, but not a hard guarantee if banding internals change.
- **"Often several times larger" in the docs** rests on the measured range of 0-12.3x.
- **Fit and banding times are rough:** load average 20-43 on the shared 32-core box.
- **Cells (deployed_census) are not monotone in banding strength**, because banding can densify factored tables (surrender split 1: 78,915 -> 119,209 at the default, 595,632 at cap x0.25). Readability comparisons use that census.
## open_questions
- **band_tolerance on small data (outside issue C).** At 8 bags, graduated, `band_tolerance` 0.375 beats 0.75: -0.095% mean / -0.048% median, 8 better / 2 worse, cells x1.90, on the 7 small fast sets (ausbi -0.36%, swmc -0.27%). The arena figure of +0.015% pooled for 0.75 may not hold on small data.
- **Multiclass banding strength.** Multiclass banding costs +0.15% to +0.48% test (graduate=False) on fr_instal and pg16_fleet at both bag counts. On fr_instal the per-class cap is the operative control at 8 bags (cap x0.25 gains 0.25-0.29% at 2.3x the cells). Whether multiclass should band less is a separate decision.
- **Honest OOB pricing.** Banding each bag's bank and scoring it on that bag's own OOB rows is the only route found to telling noise removal from signal removal. It costs n_bags banding passes and does not exist at n_bags=1. Not built.
- **Core API hardening.** In the core, `BandingRows.mse_cap = INFINITY` means "no cap", and `_TableModel.band(mse_cap=inf)` is the default. Together with sigma = inf they band without bound. Python never calls them that way now; making the core reject a non-finite budget would need the Rust gates.

## root_cause
The reported damage has two sources. Neither is the scale of the 1% check, and the fix that works is upstream of the check.

1. **The 1% check is a training-loss brake, so it cannot see benefit.** On training rows, smoothing raises deviance whether it removes noise or real shape. No training threshold can therefore tell good smoothing from bad, however it is scaled.
   - On single-output fits, training cost is anti-correlated with the test effect: corr(test change, training change) = −0.35 over 35 fits. Larger smoothing went with more benefit, mainly on swmc and ussev.
   - On fr_instal, per-table training cost predicts test harm: r = +0.72, with test cost about 45% of training cost.
   - Because the sign flips between model types, no single threshold works. Hypothesis (1) is true in scale but its remedy fails. An explained-deviance gate passes ausbi s0 (0.75% of explained, test +0.34%) and declines every swmc split (2–6% of explained), including s9 (test −0.40%).
   - Pooled over 10 splits, every tighter or explained-relative gate gives back the average benefit.
   - The native 1% check declined 1 of 76 single-output candidates, and it was the best one (swmc s9, −0.40%).

2. **The reported losses were mostly split noise.** Paired test SEs of the graduation effect are 0.02–0.35% on the small sets (swmc s0: −0.18 ± 0.29%). The 0.02% noise rule holds only for the 600k-row sets.
   - Ausbi splits 0 and 1 (+0.34%, +0.13%) were the two worst of ten. Over all ten, ausbi averages −0.015 ± 0.058%, and swmc −0.081 ± 0.054% (graduation helps there).
   - fremtpl2 averages +0.013% over two splits (+0.032 ± 0.017, −0.007 ± 0.022).

3. **The real, repeatable harm is in GCV's choice of strength, and it shows on multiclass fits.**
   - **Mechanism a, boundary minima.** GCV estimates noise from a table's own roughness. On few-cell tables, and on a rare class's noisy logit, the criterion keeps falling to the top of the grid (alpha = 3e4). The chosen strength is then set by the grid's arbitrary ceiling, not by the data, and pushes the table towards its linear trend. Examples:
     - ausbi's 5-level main effect, with 1,400–8,500 rows per cell, flattened to near-linear: +0.11% test alone.
     - fr_instal's two worst tables, rare-class main effects: +0.07% and +0.04%. One moves a 1,402-row cell from −0.50 to +0.36 in logit.
     - lifesurr's curvature-weighted catastrophe was also a ceiling choice.
   - **Mechanism b, banded axes.** Graduation runs after banding, and banding keeps only steps larger than the bag noise. With banding on, GCV smooths 3–5× more tables (fr_instal 177 vs 60–77, pg17_cover ~530 vs ~110). fr_instal harm halves with banding off: +0.61/+0.50% becomes +0.25/+0.12%.
   - Ungated, fr_instal costs +0.45 to +0.61% on all four splits, 5–7 SE. The 1% brake catches it only by scale (training ×1.014–1.016). Prudential gets through: +0.057/+0.028% at ×1.0093–1.0098.

**Hypothesis (2), curvature-weighted fidelity.** GCV gives the same smoother when every weight is scaled by one constant; only the fixed alpha grid's edges change. So the `w_scale` comment's over-smoothing argument is wrong in principle. Curvature weights are also unsafe: lifesurr has a 11,558-row cell at logit −4.0 among neighbours near +3.6. Its curvature is about 10 against about 40, so curvature-GCV pulled it onto the trend, giving +203% test.

**Hypothesis (3), out-of-bag evidence, does not work at these effect sizes.**
- Adding the deployed candidate's change to the out-of-bag score carries the same in-sample optimism as the training check: corr −0.32 with test.
- The honest version smooths each bag's tables and scores them only on that bag's out-of-bag rows. It has no predictive power: corr −0.02 over 35 fits, sign agreement 15/35. Each row is out-of-bag for only 1.6 of 8 bags.

## risks
1. **fr_instal is protected only by scale.** Under the new rule it is declined at training ×1.0114–1.0140 (HEAD ×1.014–1.016), so the margin shrank. Its residual harm sits on unbanded axes, at +0.09–0.16% when banded axes are skipped and adopted at ×1.002–1.005. A slightly milder fr_instal-like multiclass set could be adopted with harm under either rule.

2. **The single-output gain is modest.** Pooled paired −0.009 ± 0.008% (about 1 SE); lapse is the only individually significant set (−0.006 ± 0.0025). The case for the rule rests on:
   - the mechanism: a boundary choice is set by an arbitrary constant;
   - prudential: −0.044 ± 0.005, better on both splits;
   - the absence of harm.
   pg17_instal is slightly worse (+0.0037 ± 0.0024, same sign on all 4 splits, within each split's SE).

3. **Two tests were changed, not just added.**
   - test_html_report's GCV-decline test now patches `_gcv_smooth` to flatten the table. Under the new rule I could not find a natural single-table fixture that GCV smooths and the check reliably declines.
   - test_sklearn's reanchor test gains `graduate=False`. This exposes a pre-existing interaction: an adopted graduation's native log-link f0 re-anchor overrides `reanchor=False`. HEAD's test passed only because that fixture's graduation was declined; more candidates are now cheap enough to be adopted, so the override fires more often.

4. **Coverage gaps.** The evidence covers Poisson with exposure, Gamma, binary and multiclass on the cached team sets. It does not include Tweedie, squared error, grouped/panel or monotone fits. The rule lives in the shared GCV selection, so it applies to all of them.

5. **Splits are few for the slow sets.** Two each for fremtpl2 and prudential; four for the other multiclass sets.
## open_questions
1. **What causes the residual multiclass over-smoothing on fr_instal?** It is still unidentified. About 30% of the class-table variance is the softmax-redundant common component, which each class's GCV smooths along with its own shape. The class banks have different merged grids, though, so they cannot be centred cell-wise. GCV with support weights treats every class's logit cells alike, whatever the class's frequency (2.8% for class 2).

2. **Would graduating before banding help?** This order swap is untested. It would keep both stages active on interactions. I did not build it for three reasons:
   - banding's distil target would become the graduated model;
   - unbanded pairs often exceed the 2,500-cell joint cap;
   - multiclass banding runs natively inside `fit_multiclass_pruned`.

3. **Should graduation respect `reanchor=False`?** An adopted graduation re-anchors log-link f0 regardless. The alternative is to report the override in `binding_report_` as OVERRIDDEN.

4. **`graduation_validation_` is inconsistent when nothing is smoothed.** It lacks `skipped` and `candidate_count` on the early return in `_graduate_table_model` (norway shows `adopted: False` with no reason), while the later no-update path sets them. Not changed here, because it would reword the monotone tests that assert `"skipped" not in graduation_validation_`.

5. **The `w_scale` comment in `_graduate_table_model` is wrong in principle.** It says row-count GCV over-smooths by the mean weight. GCV picks the same smoother if every weight is scaled by one constant, apart from the fixed alpha grid's edges, so `w_scale` only relocates the grid. It does carry the fixed-alpha semantics, so I left the code and the comment alone.

6. **The out-of-bag acceptance prototypes are in the results dir** if anyone wants to revisit them on larger sets, where effects exceed the jury's resolution.

## root_cause
Two causes. Neither is the one the issue names.

(1) The reported harm comes from a categorical-encoder leak, not from trees overfitting the slice. pg17_instal adopted step 0.75 and lost 0.21%/0.41% test cross-entropy. A categorical with more levels than cat_direct_max_levels is trained on k-fold cross-fit target statistics. Every post-fit stage, however, bins the training rows through the frozen full-data map, and that map's statistic for a level contains its rows' own targets: a level whose rows happen to sit high is binned high. A re-solve fitted to the training residual on those cells learns the leak; training deviance fell 1.08% and 1.05%. Every slice of training rows endorses it, out-of-bag or not, because each row's own target placed it. Evidence:
- The issue's remedy was built and measured. With the residual and the check both taken from the bags' out-of-bag jury, restricted to the keep-set, pg17_instal still adopts step 0.75 and loses 0.21% and 0.52%. For the shipped correction, the out-of-bag slice predicted -0.50%/-0.57% and the in-sample slice -0.42%/-0.46%, while test went +0.21%/+0.41%. The out-of-bag curve's minimum sat at the same step as the in-sample one on every multiclass fit.
- Causal test: with the shipped check unchanged, leaving tables on target-encoded categoricals as fitted (vh_model 979 levels, pol_insee_code 16,303, vh_make 97) turns pg17_instal into -0.021%/-0.015%. The training drop shrinks to 0.13%/0.03%, and both checks then agree with test.
- The harm appears exactly where such categoricals exist. ussev (CLASS, 18 levels) is the only fast single-output set with one, and the only one the rebalance hurt (+0.13% to +0.22% on 3 of 5 splits). Applying its correction unconditionally costs +0.16% with CLASS's tables re-solved and +0.06% without; split 4 goes from +0.39% to -0.03%. Sets without such a categorical show no harm: swmc, ausbi, lapse, lifesurr, surrender, norway, pg16_fleet, fr_instal.

(2) On single-output fits the stage did almost nothing because the slice check is biased against the re-solve, not optimistic. The soup trained on the slice rows, so a correction fitted to the other rows' residual is anti-correlated with the slice's own noise. Out-of-bag juries do not fix this, because the jury's bags also trained on the slice.
- On the 25 single-output dataset-splits without a leaky categorical, applying the correction unconditionally lowers test deviance by 0.13% (in-sample residual) or 0.18% (out-of-bag residual) on average. The shipped check adopted it on 6 of 30 fits, for a mean change of +0.005%.
- Example, ausbi s0: the correction gains 0.59% on test while both slices predicted +0.57% (worse). The out-of-bag-residual correction gains 1.39% while its own out-of-bag slice predicted +0.98%.
- A bag never saw its own out-of-bag rows. Re-solving each bag on its in-bag rows and scoring it on its out-of-bag rows is therefore an honest check: it adopts on 28 of 30 fits, for a mean of -0.11%.

## recommendation
Ship the full change in final_patch.diff.

What it does:
1. A categorical whose training rows are binned by cross-fit target statistics is left as fitted and reported in pruning_report_["rebalance"]["excluded"]. That means more levels than cat_direct_max_levels; binary, direct-bypass and count channels are kept.

## open_questions
- **The serve-map leak almost certainly reaches other stages.** The ranked path's out-of-bag prefix deviances, banding's curvature-weighted cells and graduation's cell weights all score training rows through the full-data target-statistic map. Target-encoded tables therefore look better than they are at selection time too. The root fix would score post-fit stages on the training-time cross-fit encodings; that would also let the rebalance re-solve those tables. It is plumbing beyond this issue.
- **Can the check get cheaper without losing honesty?**
  - Share the per-bag keep-restricted scores with banding's bag_score_variance, which builds the same bag banks.
  - Deploy the mean of the bags' corrections instead of a separate all-rows solve, which saves one solve per class and deploys exactly what was judged.

## summary
The accuracy case survives independent checking.
- On fresh multiclass splits the recommended change reproduces in size: pg17_instal s2 goes from HEAD +0.216% to final -0.059% vs no rebalance, and fr_instal s2 from -0.055% to -0.241%. Both mechanisms work: the leak exclusion stops the harm, and the per-bag check lets the re-solve through where it helps.
- Single-output gains are modest and depend on the dataset: about -0.09% at dataset level, about -0.05% without ausbi, and small to nil on fresh splits of the investigator's datasets. They are clearly positive on a new wide binary task (-0.09% to -0.20%).

The cost claim is wrong for wide data: single-output fits slow by 22-25%, not 5-6%. Peak memory rises 11-14% and was never measured. The single-output check is effectively always-adopt (28/30) and is insurance rather than the source of the gain. The ussev part of the root-cause story is not supported.

Several patch defects need fixing before landing:
- A silent unchecked adopt when no bag leaves a row out.
- No membership-length check.
- An honesty claim that is false under cell_refit_base.
- Docs that describe the guard as checking the deployed values.

Accept with changes. Land the leak exclusion separately and first. Restate the measured cost, and let the maintainer decide on the per-bag check (above all its single-output half) with those numbers in hand. Fix the small defects and docs, and log the follow-ups: the serve-map leak in other stages, an honest n_bags=1 check, and a sharper leak criterion.
